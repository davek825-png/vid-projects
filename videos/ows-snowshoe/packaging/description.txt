Every winter the road over the Sierra Nevada disappeared under snow, and the mail stopped. When a notice ran in 1855 asking for a mail carrier, a Norwegian immigrant named John "Snowshoe" Thompson took the job nobody else could do: 90 miles over the mountains on ten-foot wooden skis, carrying 60 to 80 pounds of mail.
For 20 winters the letters got through. He pulled a freezing prospector out of the snow and crossed the range again to bring back anesthesia. Then he asked to be paid, and Congress said no.

Drawn from period newspaper accounts (the Sacramento Union), the 1869 Nevada Legislature's request to Congress, and a 2005 public television investigation.
