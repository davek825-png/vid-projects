In 1855 the U.S. Army sent men across the ocean and brought 33 camels back to Texas, along with the handlers who knew how to manage them. Horses and mules kept dying on the harsh desert supply routes of the American Southwest, so the Army got creative.
In 1857 Edward Beale led the camels on his wagon road survey across the Jornada del Muerto to Los Angeles. Then the Civil War came, Camp Verde fell to the Confederacy, and the camels were auctioned, sent to zoos and circuses, or simply wandered off into the desert.
